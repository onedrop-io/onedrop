---
title: 'Publish a project'
description: 'Give a project its own URL, private to your team or public to anyone.'
---

Publishing gives a project its own address. On a [server install](/install#install-on-a-server) it can be on your own domain, such as `https://timesheets-4.onedrop.example.com`. Anywhere, including a laptop, it can be on your team's [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com) network, such as `https://timesheets-4.your-tailnet.ts.net`. Where hosting is set up, it can also be deployed to [Hosting](/guides/hosting), where it runs on its own.

<Info>
    Publishing to your domain or Tailscale shares the live sandbox, including
    changes the agent is still making. Publishing to [Hosting](/guides/hosting)
    deploys a separate copy that only changes when you republish.
</Info>

## Publish

<Steps>
    <Step title="Open the Publish panel">
        Click **Publish** at the top right of the workspace.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Choose where">
        On a server, pick **Your domain** or **Tailscale**. Where hosting is set
        up, **Hosting** is offered too. Otherwise it's Tailscale.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Choose who can open it">
        **Private** or **Public** (see the table below).
    </Step>
    <Step title="Click Publish">
        The status changes to **Publishing…** and then shows the URL with a copy
        button. You can close the panel: the **Publish** button spins and says
        **Publishing…** until it's done. You can close the panel: the **Publish** button spins and says
        **Publishing…** until it's done.
    </Step>
</Steps>

|             | Your domain                         | Tailscale                                              |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Private** | Anyone in the project's organization | People on your team's tailnet (Tailscale Serve)        |
| **Public**  | Anyone on the internet with the URL | Anyone on the internet with the URL (Tailscale Funnel) |

To serve it at a domain you own, see [Custom domains](/guides/domains).

Someone who opens a private app on your domain without being signed in is sent to sign in to OneDrop first, then back to the page they asked for. They need to be in the project's [organization](/guides/organizations), but don't need to set up an AI or be part of the project.

If OneDrop has no Tailscale auth key configured, publishing to Tailscale shows **Approve in Tailscale**. Open the link, approve the device in your Tailscale account, and publishing continues on its own. You only approve each project once.

## Waiting for a review

On OneDrop's hosted service, apps are checked before they go public, to keep out phishing pages, scams and malware. The check looks at the project's name, its prompts, and the app's home page, and takes a moment while the status says **Publishing…**. Almost every app goes live straight away.

If the check thinks an app might be one of those, the status says **Waiting for a review** and the app stays offline until someone at OneDrop looks at it. It goes live by itself once approved; you can keep building meanwhile, and publishing privately still works. An app that's taken down can't be published publicly or shared again.

Apps already published publicly are checked again, at most once a day, after the agent changes them.

## Republish or unpublish

Once published, the button says **Republish**. Use it to switch between Private and Public, or between your domain and Tailscale (the project comes off the one it was on). **Unpublish** takes the project offline.

The **Tools → Publishing** section shows the same status, URL, and who published it.

## Works with any framework

Published URLs reach your app as `localhost`, so frameworks that block unknown hostnames (such as Vite and Next.js dev servers) work without extra configuration.

Apps that refuse to be shown in frames (Rails and Django send `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN` by default) still show in the preview: OneDrop drops that header, and a `frame-ancestors` policy, for the preview only. Apps whose own code refuses to run in a frame (NocoDB, for one) work too: the preview rewrites those checks, so the app behaves as it does in its own tab. Your published URL keeps all of them.

Setting up Tailscale for your server is covered in [Tailscale publishing](/self-hosting/tailscale).
