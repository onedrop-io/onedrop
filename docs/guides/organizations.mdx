An organization is the boundary between companies. Its projects, groups, invites and shared skills are only seen by the people in it. Your profile, sign-in methods, SSH keys and AI connections belong to you and come with you to every organization you're in.

## Your own install

A self-hosted OneDrop has one organization, named after the install. Everyone who signs up joins it, and the first account owns it. Admins run it, so nothing changes for a single company.

## The hosted install

On the hosted OneDrop each company has its own organization:

- Signing up gives you a new organization that you own, named after you.
- Signing up through an [invite](/guides/invite-people) puts you in the inviter's organization instead.
- Signing up with a work email at a domain your company's organization has verified (see [Email domains](#email-domains)) puts you in that organization instead, once you verify your email.
- You can belong to more than one organization, and create more: click your name at the bottom of the sidebar, open **Organization** (it shows the organization's name) and choose **Create organization**. You own the new one, and its address is made from its name.

## Which organization you're in

The address says which organization a page belongs to:

| Address                                     | Organization                         |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `/o/<slug>`                                 | That organization's new-project page |
| `/o/<slug>/apps`                            | Its [published apps](/guides/apps)   |
| `/o/<slug>/groups`, `/o/<slug>/invitations` | Its groups and invites               |
| `/projects/<id>`                            | The project's organization           |

The organization you're in shows under your name at the bottom of the sidebar. Click your name and open the organization's submenu, shown with its logo (or, without one, its initial on the hosted install and your install's logo on your own), to switch to another organization (you land on its new-project page) or open its settings.

The sidebar lists the projects of the organization you're in, and new projects go into it. Opening an organization's page or one of its projects makes it your current organization, so signing in takes you back there. Two browser tabs can be in two different organizations.

## What stays inside an organization

- **Projects.** Only their owner and the organization's owners and admins can open them. Their published apps are listed for everyone in it on [Apps](/guides/apps). If you leave an organization, you lose access to the projects you made there; they stay with the organization.
- **Groups.** You can only add people from the same organization.
- **Shared skills.** [Sharing a skill](/guides/agent-skills) shares it with everyone in its organization.
- **Sign in with OneDrop.** [Your app's OneDrop sign-in](/guides/users-and-auth) only lets in people from the project's organization.
- **Private apps.** An app [published](/guides/publish) as Private on your domain opens for people in the project's organization.

Anything from another organization shows as not found, so nobody can tell whether it exists.

## Roles

| Role       | Can                                                                                                                         |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Owner**  | Everything an admin can, and make, change or remove owners                                                                  |
| **Admin**  | Open and manage every project, group and invite, rename the organization, change who's a member or admin, and see its usage |
| **Member** | Work on their own projects and join groups                                                                                  |

An organization always keeps at least one owner.

## Manage your organization

Open **Organization** under **People** in [Settings](/guides/settings). Everyone in the organization sees its members, their roles, and when they joined. Owners and admins can also:

- **Give it a logo.** PNG, JPEG, WebP or SVG, up to 1 MB. It shows in the account menu's organization submenu, only to its members. Remove it to go back to its initial.
- **Rename it, or change its address.** The address is the `/o/<slug>` part of its pages: lowercase letters, numbers and dashes. Links to the old address stop working.
- **Change roles** with the menu next to each person.
- **Limit task copies.** Under **Task copies**, set the most [task copies](/guides/tasks#each-tasks-own-copy-of-the-app) one of its projects runs at once; each is a sandbox that costs money to run. Leave it empty for no limit of its own. When the install has a lower limit, that one applies.
- **Share secrets with its projects** under **Secrets**: environment variables like an AWS profile, for all projects or selected ones. See [Organization secrets](/guides/secrets#organization-secrets).
- **Connect its own hosting accounts** (Fly.io, Cloudflare, Neon, Upstash) under **Hosting**, so its apps are [hosted](/guides/hosting#use-your-organizations-own-accounts) there and billed to it.
- **Remove someone** (hosted install only). They leave the organization's groups and lose its projects right away; the projects stay in the organization. Anyone can **Leave** an organization the same way.

On your own install everyone stays in its one organization. To take someone out, an admin deletes their account.

## Organization usage
## Email domains

On the hosted install, owners and admins can let everyone with a work email join the organization, without sending invites. In **Settings → Organization → Email domains**:

1. Enter your company's email domain (for example `acme.com`) and click **Add domain**.
2. Add the TXT record it shows (`onedrop-verification=…`) to that domain where its DNS is managed. Leave the name empty, or use `@`, so the record is on the domain itself.
3. Click **Check**. DNS changes can take a few minutes to show up; check again if it isn't found yet.

Once the domain shows **Verified**:

- People who sign up with an email at that domain join the organization as members, once they've verified their email.
- People who already have an account with that email see **Join Acme** in the **Organization** submenu, and join with one click.
- Only that exact domain counts: `eu.acme.com` is a domain of its own. Add it too if you need it.
- Only one organization can verify a domain.
- Signing up through an [invite](/guides/invite-people) still joins the inviter's organization.

Removing a domain keeps everyone who joined through it. While a domain is verified, anyone with an email there can join again, even after being removed; to stop that, remove the domain.


Owners and admins see a usage link named after the organization in the account menu (for example **Acme usage**). It's the [Usage](/guides/usage) page for every agent run in the organization's projects, deleted projects included, with a **Person** breakdown showing whose runs they were.

Platform admins run the install itself (see [Administer OneDrop](/self-hosting/administration)). On the hosted install that doesn't give them access to a company's projects.
